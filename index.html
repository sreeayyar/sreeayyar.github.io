Migrating in the Face of Climate Change: Assessing the Role of Anti-Poverty Programs

with Sarah Winton · Primary data collection complete. Analysis underway.

Abstract

Macro models estimate high migration flows in response to climate change. However, whether and why individual households migrate in response to slow-onset climate stress is an open question. This project studies the climate-driven migration decisions of ultra-poor households in Upper Egypt, who are among the most climate-vulnerable, and the role of anti-poverty programs in these decisions. Building on an existing RCT of an anti-poverty Graduation program, we survey over 7,500 men and women from ultra-poor rural households five-years after rollout. We overlay high-resolution, satellite-based heat and drought measures on our surveys, and harmonise them with the RCT's baseline, midline, and two endline household surveys. Comparing households who were equally poor at baseline, before 50% receive an asset transfer and another 25% receive a transfer of half the value, reveals a non-monotonic relationship between asset transfer values and migration outcomes. We discuss our results in the context of migration as a coping versus investment strategy.

Imagine your Life at 25: Children's Gender Attitudes and Later-Life Outcomes

with Uta Bolt, Eric French, and Cormac O'Dea

Revise & Resubmit, JPE Microeconomics · [Draft]

Abstract

Using thousands of essays written by 11-year-olds in 1969, we construct an index measuring children's conformity to gender norms. We link this index to outcomes over the life-cycle. Conditional on age-11 covariates, a one standard deviation increase in our index predicts a 4% decline in lifetime earnings for girls, associated with lower wages and fewer hours worked. We find no statistically significant association for boys. Education, occupation, and family formation account for 40% of the earnings decline observed among girls.

Conditional Likelihood Ratio Test with Many Weak Instruments

with Taisuke Otsu and Yukitoshi Matsushita

Econometric Theory, 2025 · [Published Version]

Abstract

This article extends the validity of the conditional likelihood ratio (CLR) test developed by Moreira (2003) to instrumental variable regression models with unknown homoskedastic error variance and many weak instruments. We argue that the conventional CLR test with estimated error variance loses exact similarity and is asymptotically invalid in this setting. We propose a modified critical value function for the likelihood ratio (LR) statistic with estimated error variance, and prove that our modified test achieves asymptotic validity under many weak instruments asymptotics. Our critical value function is constructed by representing the LR using four statistics, instead of two as in Moreira (2003). A simulation study illustrates the desirable finite sample properties of our test.
